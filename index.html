Who Gets Lost in Recovery?

University of Georgia · Team 5Sigma (5 members) · R, Python, Quarto

Stormont Vail Health gave DataFest teams over 7 million patient encounters and asked us to map the patient journey. We narrowed in on fractures, because their diagnosis codes record each stage of recovery, and flagged patients whose healing went wrong. Smoking and age 65+ raised the odds of a complication as expected. Married patients and MyChart users also had more recorded complications, which we traced to them staying in the system long enough to be diagnosed, while disconnected patients quietly dropped out.

My role: Identified why to focus on bone injuries, interpreted the chi-squared and logistic regression results, built the presentation, and presented to the judges.

Code is in a private team repository.

The Gender Wage Gap Over a Career

UGA Economics coursework · R · Census CPS data

Using 46,000+ full-time workers from the March 2022 Current Population Survey, I estimated a 15% hourly wage gap between women and men of the same age. Controlling for education made it grow to 21%, since women in the sample were more likely to hold a college degree. The gap roughly doubles over the first ten years of a career.

Are Hermès Bags a Good Investment?

Client recommendation · R · 1,255 auction sales

The average auction price for Birkin and Kelly bags rose over time, but a bag-level regression showed comparable bags actually lost about 3.7% of their value per year. The rising average came from more rare Himalaya and diamond-hardware bags being sold. My recommendation: don't count on appreciation, and only buy the rarest pieces.

Predicting Home Prices in Ames, Iowa

Predictive modeling · R · OLS, lasso, ridge

I compared five regression models plus lasso and ridge on 460 homes the models had never seen. The model with the best in-sample fit overfit, while a smaller model cut the typical prediction error to about $32,800. The lesson: test on held-out data before trusting R².

Do Betting Odds Predict Soccer Results?

SQL + Python · 22,592 European league matches

I used SQL to join match, league, and team tables, then turned Bet365's odds into probabilities in Python. The odds are accurate for most matches, but heavy favorites win 4 to 7 points more often than implied while long shots win about a quarter less often. That bias still isn't enough to beat the bookmaker's ~6% margin, and the Premier League has the lowest markup at 4.2%.
